Spindle · parser design

The parser has two sides: the model, and you.

The AI extracts everything it's confident about — silently, automatically, no work for you. When it's unsure, it doesn't guess. It asks one quick question and you tap an answer. When it's truly lost, it shows you the likely candidates and lets you pick. Every answer you give teaches it your brand's vocabulary, so it asks less every week.

~80%
Confident → silent
Style, stage and facts are clear. Filed automatically. You never see it unless you look.
~15%
Unsure → one tap
It has a best guess but wants you to confirm. Two seconds, one tap. This is where trust is built.
~5%
Lost → you pick
It can't tell which style. Shows the likely candidates and asks you to choose. Honest, not silent.

The questions get rarer every week.

Each answer is training data for your brand. The parser learns that "the khaki jacket" means 6601234, that Vikram's "260gsm" emails are about fabric substitutions, that Arjun calls the Cobalt dress "the blue one." What needs your eyes in week one is filed silently by week six.

Share of emails that need a confirmation, illustrative. The line bends because corrections compound — the parser is onboarding itself to how your suppliers actually write.

Why this matters: a parser that guesses silently has to be right ~95% of the time or merchandisers stop trusting it — and they can't tell which answers to doubt. A parser that asks when unsure earns trust at 80%, because it never pretends. The two-sided design isn't a crutch for a weak model. It's what makes the model safe to rely on.
